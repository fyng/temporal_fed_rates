# Economist writing style

Checklist-based. Apply every applicable rule before shipping any user-facing text.

## The six rules

1. Never use a metaphor, simile or other figure of speech which you are used to seeing in print.
2. Never use a long word where a short one will do.
3. If it is possible to cut out a word, always cut it out.
4. Never use the passive where you can use the active.
5. Never use a foreign phrase, a scientific word or a jargon word if you can think of an everyday English equivalent.
6. Break any of these rules sooner than say anything outright barbarous.

## Before you write

- Clarity of writing follows clarity of thought: think what you want to say, then say it as simply as possible.
- Ask of every sentence: What am I trying to say? What words will express it? Could I put it more shortly?
- Catch the reader's attention, then get straight in: no clearing your throat, setting the scene, or sketching background.
- An article has a beginning, a middle and an end; it should suffer if even one sentence is cut.

## Tone

- Do not be stuffy: use the language of everyday speech, not of spokesmen, lawyers or bureaucrats.
- Do not be hectoring or arrogant: show, don't assert; go easy on oughts and shoulds.
- Do not be too pleased with yourself: never boast of a correct prediction.
- Do not be too chatty: no "surprise, surprise", no "wait for it".
- Do not be too didactic: don't open sentences with Compare, Consider, Note, Remember.
- Simple sentences help; long paragraphs confuse; one-sentence paragraphs only occasionally.

## Don't verb nouns, don't noun verbs

- Do not verb nouns or noun adjectives.
- Verbs to avoid: access (files), action (proposals), author (books), critique, gun down, haemorrhage (red ink), impact, loan, pressure, progress (reports), reference, source (inputs), summit (a hill), trial (programmes).
- Nouned adjectives to avoid: advisory (prefer warning), centennial (prefer centenary), inaugural (prefer inauguration).
- Write "an attempted coup", not "a coup attempt"; "a suspected terrorist", not "a terrorist suspect".
- Don't stack nouns into adjectival strings ("Texas millionaire real-estate developer and failed thrift entrepreneur").

## Numbers, units, dates

- Use m for million, bn for billion, trn for trillion.
- Lower case, no space when attached to figures: 15kg, 100mph, 4.30pm.
- Two abbreviations together must be separated: 60m b/d.
- Spell out page, pages, hectares, miles.
- Write % in charts and tables; in running prose per cent is acceptable but % is preferred in this repo for brevity.
- Dates in prose are British-style: August 8th 2003, no comma.
- Do not use Fahrenheit; do not spell out Centigrade.

## Abbreviations

- Spell out on first appearance unless the abbreviation is more familiar than the full form (GDP, IMF, OECD, HIV).
- No initials for an organisation mentioned only once.
- After first mention prefer "the Fed", "the central bank", "the committee" to repeating the acronym.
- Prefer boss or chief executive to CEO.
- FOMC: spell out Federal Open Market Committee on first use, then "the committee" or "the Fed"; federal funds rate on first use, then "the fed-funds rate"; prefer "raise/cut rates" to "tighten/loosen policy" unless the distinction matters.

## British usage

- the army, not the military; company, not corporation; court, not courtroom; normality, not normalcy.
- programme (except a computer program); among, not amongst; while, not whilst.
- raise cattle, bring up children; passengers, not riders; railway station, not train station.
- Put adverbs after the verb.
- Don't shy from the perfect tense: "the Fed has raised rates" unless you name a date.

## Chart and headline copy

- Chart headline: short, bold, often a pun or play on words; 2-4 words; it is a label, not a summary — "Less cash, less credit", "The money drain", "Mexican waves".
- Subtitle states what is measured and its units, sentence case, no full stop: "United States, federal funds target rate, %".
- Put units and date range on a second line at smaller size where possible.
- Source line: "Source: FRED" / "Sources: FRED; The Economist" — semicolons between sources, no full stop.
- Footnote symbols in order: * † ‡ § ** †† ‡‡ §§.
- Axis labels: no repetition of the units already given in the subtitle.
- Never put a full stop at the end of a title, subtitle, axis label or source line.

## Checklist before shipping

- Cut every unnecessary word.
- Use the active voice.
- Remove every stale metaphor.
- Explain every abbreviation on first use.
- Put no full stops in chart furniture.
- Read it aloud.
